**5. Purposes**

Owner's wording of 2026-11-14, drafted for Path A:

1. To advance public education in cybersecurity, artificial intelligence literacy, digital-investigations literacy, digital safety, information integrity, and transparent systems knowledge.
2. To research, document, publish, and make freely available non-commercial educational frameworks, guides, reports, and resources that help the public understand digital risk, AI systems, cybersecurity practices, privacy, and system transparency.
3. To organize and deliver educational programs, workshops, public briefings, community learning sessions, and open educational resources for students, community organizations, small organizations, and the general public in Canada.
4. To collaborate with educators, researchers, libraries, community organizations, and civil-society groups on public-interest digital safety and AI-literacy initiatives.
5. To do all things incidental or ancillary to the foregoing purposes, provided that the corporation shall not carry on any activity for the private profit of its members, directors, officers, or related parties.

Notes for counsel:

- On this wording the purposes are the advancement of education, which makes the Institute a charitable corporation under the Act from incorporation [L-29]. Under Path B they must be rewritten. [LEGAL REVIEW]
- Purposes 4 (collaborate) and 5 (incidental and ancillary) read as activities and powers, not purposes. If a charity application is planned, the CRA will test each purpose on its own. Ask whether 4 and 5 should move to a powers clause (question A7).
- Purpose 2 includes research. Ask whether it needs to say that research results are published, so that the purpose stays educational.
- "Related parties" in purpose 5 is not defined in the articles. Ask whether to define it or drop it.

**6. Classes of members**

One class. Each member has one vote at every meeting of members [L-41]. Who may be a member is set in the by-law (By-law No. 1, article 4.2) [L-35].

No share capital entry is needed. A corporation under the Act is a body corporate without share capital [L-43].

**7. Special provisions**

Kept from the earlier sheet:

- The corporation shall be carried on without the purpose of gain for its members, and any profits or other accretions to the corporation shall be used in promoting its purposes.
- Directors serve without remuneration for acting as directors. A director may be reimbursed for reasonable expenses incurred for the corporation.
- No part of the corporation's income is payable to, or otherwise available for the personal benefit of, any member or director.

Proposed in the owner's kit of 2026-11-14, for decision:

- The corporation shall not issue receipts for income tax purposes unless and until it is registered as a charity under the Income Tax Act (Canada). This repeats the law [L-20] and By-law No. 1, article 10.8. Because it is conditional, it does not need amending once the Institute is registered.
- The corporation shall keep its own books, accounts, bank accounts, contracts and records, separate from those of ClearGlass Inc. Putting this in the articles means only a members' special resolution can remove it [L-42]. That is the reason to include it. The cost is the same: it cannot be relaxed quickly if circumstances change. [DECISION REQUIRED] whether either provision goes into the articles or stays in the by-law and policies only.

Not adopted: the owner's kit would allow "compensation approved in accordance with applicable law" for directors. The earlier sheet bars paying directors for acting as directors. Charity law also limits paying directors of a charitable corporation [L-30], so the stricter wording stays. [LEGAL REVIEW]

Question A9 remains open: which special provisions a charitable corporation's articles must contain.

**8. Distribution of property on dissolution**

Path A wording, not yet cleared:

> On the dissolution or winding up of the corporation, and after payment of all its debts and liabilities, its remaining property shall be distributed or disposed of only to one or more Canadian bodies corporate that are registered charities under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and that have purposes similar to those of the corporation. No part of the remaining property shall be distributed to a member, director or officer of the corporation.

Why this wording:

- The earlier sheet, and the owner's kit of 2026-11-14, send the property to "qualified donees". For a charitable corporation the Act allows a narrower group: a Canadian body corporate that is a registered charity with similar purposes, the Crown in right of Ontario or of Canada or an agent of either, or a municipality in Canada [L-19].
- The clause above uses only the first of those. The Crown, its agents and municipalities are left out until counsel confirms that each also meets the CRA's requirement for a registered charity. [LEGAL REVIEW]
- A non-charitable public benefit corporation may give to any recipient on that list [L-19], and an ordinary corporation follows its articles. So the same clause is valid whichever of those the Institute turns out to be. Counsel to confirm (question A8).

**ClearGlass Institute: proposed Ontario not-for-profit corporation for public-interest digital education**
**Registered office (proposed):** Burlington, Ontario, Canada
**Prepared for:** the founding directors and their advisers

## Why it is being created

People, community groups and small organizations now depend on AI systems and online services they have no practical way to evaluate. Most of the explanatory material they can find is written by companies selling something. ClearGlass Institute is proposed as a source of plain-language, cited, non-commercial education that helps the public understand digital risk and make its own judgments.

## Mission and who it serves

**Mission:** to advance public education in digital safety, AI literacy, cybersecurity awareness, digital-investigations literacy, information integrity, and transparent systems knowledge.

**Intended beneficiaries:** students, community organizations, small organizations, and the general public in Canada. The benefit is public. Members and directors receive nothing from it.

**First program:** Public AI and Cyber Literacy Briefs. Short, sourced explainers, free to read, with no sign-up.

## Why it is separate from ClearGlass Inc.

ClearGlass Inc. is a commercial company that builds products and services. The Institute educates and publishes. Keeping them apart is what makes the Institute's material credible, and it is what funders and regulators will check first. The separation is structural:

- its own corporation, board, bank account, accounting records, domain, and contracts;
- no referrals, lead collection, or product promotion for ClearGlass Inc.;
- every dealing with ClearGlass Inc. disclosed, minuted, and on written fair-market terms, with conflicted directors out of the room [L-32];
- public-education material stays available for public use.

## What is true today

- ClearGlass Institute has not been incorporated. No certificate, business number, or bank account exists.
- ClearGlass Institute is not a registered charity. It cannot issue official donation receipts, and incorporation alone will not change that [L-20].
- Nothing has been published, and no organization has agreed to work with it.

## The decision that comes before filing

[DECISION REQUIRED] Ontario's Act defines a charitable corporation as one incorporated for, among other things, the advancement of education, and registration with the Canada Revenue Agency is not part of that definition [L-29]. The proposed purposes are educational. On that wording the Institute would be a charitable corporation under Ontario law from its first day, with charity-law duties from that day [L-30], while having no charitable registration for tax purposes. The federal exemption for non-profit organizations is for bodies that are not charities [L-23].

The founders have to choose, on legal and tax advice, between two consistent paths:

- **Path A.** Keep the educational purposes, accept Ontario charity-law duties from day one, and apply to the CRA for registration soon after incorporation.
- **Path B.** Rewrite the purposes so they are not charitable, operate as an ordinary non-profit, and give up donation receipts and funders that require registered status.

This package is drafted for Path A, because the mission is public education funded by grants and gifts. [LEGAL REVIEW] before any filing. See `13_OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`.

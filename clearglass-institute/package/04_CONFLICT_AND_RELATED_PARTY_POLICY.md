## CLEARGLASS INSTITUTE
## Conflict-of-Interest and Related-Party Policy

**Why this policy exists.** A founder of the Institute controls ClearGlass Inc., a commercial company working in the same field under part of the same name. This policy keeps the two apart and makes every dealing between them visible and recorded.

**1. Who is covered.** Every director, officer, employee, and volunteer who can influence a decision of the Institute.

**2. Definitions.**

- A **conflict of interest** exists when a covered person's own interest, or the interest of a related party, could affect, or could reasonably appear to affect, that person's judgment on a matter before the Institute.
- A **related party** is: ClearGlass Inc. and any entity it controls or that controls it; a covered person; that person's spouse or partner, parent, child, sibling or household member; and any business in which any of them is a director or officer or holds a material interest.

**3. Disclosure.** Each covered person signs a disclosure of related parties on joining and once a year. A covered person who has an interest in a matter says so before it is discussed, and the disclosure is entered in the minutes. The founder's interest in ClearGlass Inc. is recorded as a standing disclosure in the minutes of the first meeting.

**4. Recusal.** A director with an interest in a contract or transaction leaves the meeting for the discussion and the vote, and does not vote. The remaining directors decide. If every director has an interest, the matter goes to the members.

**5. Board approval and fair-market rationale.** The Institute enters into a contract or transaction with a related party only if the directors who have no interest approve it by resolution and the minutes record:

- what the transaction is and who the related party is;
- why the Institute needs it;
- the alternatives considered, with at least two comparison quotes or a written reason none could be obtained;
- why the terms are no less favourable to the Institute than fair market value;
- the written agreement that sets out the terms.

**6. Prohibited.** No covered person and no related party gives or receives, in connection with the Institute:

- an undisclosed payment, fee, commission or referral benefit;
- a loan to or from the Institute without the approval in section 5;
- preferential treatment in hiring, purchasing, licensing, or access to the Institute's programs, audiences or data;
- a gift worth more than $50 from anyone doing or seeking business with the Institute.

**7. Separation from ClearGlass Inc.** The Institute keeps separate from ClearGlass Inc.:

- **bank accounts**, with no joint or shared account;
- **accounting records**, with no shared ledger and no expense of one recorded in the other;
- **branding**, using the full name ClearGlass Institute in every public statement and never ClearGlass Inc.'s logo, slogans or sales material;
- **domains** and email addresses;
- **contracts**, each signed by the entity that is party to it.

The Institute does not refer anyone to ClearGlass Inc., collect contact details for it, recommend its products or services, or share personal information with it.

**8. Money from ClearGlass Inc.** Money or services from ClearGlass Inc. reach the Institute only as a documented donation, grant or sponsorship, or as a loan on written fair-market terms, each approved under section 5. Nothing is transferred as "capital". Services given free of charge are recorded in the minutes with an estimate of their value.

**9. Intellectual property.**

- Material the Institute creates for public education stays available for public-benefit use. The Institute publishes it under an open licence [DECISION REQUIRED: CC BY 4.0 or another licence] and grants no one an exclusive licence to it.
- Commercial product intellectual property belongs to ClearGlass Inc. and stays with it unless it is separately and properly licensed to the Institute in writing under section 5.
- A person who works for both keeps a record of which organization each piece of work was for. [LEGAL REVIEW] ownership of work made by people who serve both.

**10. Register and reporting.** The Secretary keeps a register of related-party transactions showing the date, the party, the subject, the value, the directors who withdrew, and the minute reference. The board reports the number and total value of related-party transactions to the members each year.

**11. Breach.** A suspected breach is reported to the Chair, or to the Treasurer if the Chair is involved. The directors who have no interest review it, record the outcome in the minutes, and may require a transaction to be reversed at the related party's cost.

**12. Review.** The board reviews this policy each year at its first meeting after the annual meeting of members.

## Notes

- Sections 3 and 4 restate what the Act already requires of a director or officer with an interest in a material contract or transaction: disclosure, withdrawal from the discussion and the vote, a deemed quorum of the remaining directors, and members' approval when every director is conflicted [L-32]. The policy applies the same rule to officers, employees and volunteers and to matters below the Act's "material" threshold.
- The Act makes an exception for a contract with an affiliate. Whether ClearGlass Inc. would be an affiliate of the Institute was not analysed. [LEGAL REVIEW] This policy applies the stricter rule either way.
- With three directors, one of whom is the founder, section 4 leaves two directors to decide every ClearGlass Inc. matter. That is workable only if both have no interest in ClearGlass Inc. See the board composition question in `13_OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`.
- If the Institute is a charitable corporation, charity law adds its own limits on benefits to directors and related persons [L-30]. [LEGAL REVIEW]

## 14.3 Which charity category the Institute would fall into

**Confirmed rule.** A registered charity is either a charitable organization or a charitable foundation, and a foundation is either public or private [L-44] [L-45]. A charitable organization must carry on its own charitable activities, or make qualifying disbursements, and meet two tests. More than 50 per cent of its directors and officers must deal at arm's length with each other and with any person, or non-arm's-length group, that has contributed more than 50 per cent of its capital. It also must not be controlled, in any manner whatever, by such a person or group [L-44]. A public foundation must meet the same two tests. A charitable foundation that fails them is a private foundation [L-45].

**Why this matters here (risk assessment).** The founder controls ClearGlass Inc., and ClearGlass Inc. may pay the early costs (doc 13, B10). If the founder and ClearGlass Inc. together supply most of the Institute's money, the board test and the control test are measured against them.

| Board | Who supplies most of the money | Likely effect (for counsel to confirm) |
|---|---|---|
| Two of three directors deal at arm's length with the founder and ClearGlass Inc. | No person or group supplies more than half | Fits a charitable organization if the Institute runs its own programs |
| Two of three directors deal at arm's length | The founder and ClearGlass Inc. supply more than half | The board test is likely met; the control test turns on facts. [LEGAL REVIEW] (doc 13, A14) |
| Only one of three directors deals at arm's length | Any | The board test fails, so the Institute cannot be a charitable organization or a public foundation; it would be a private foundation [L-45] |

Private foundations face additional rules, which were not read for this package. [LEGAL REVIEW] which apply, and whether they matter for an education program.

**Recommendations.**

1. A majority of directors deal at arm's length with the founder and ClearGlass Inc. This settles doc 13, B3: two independent directors, not one.
2. Track contributions from the founder and ClearGlass Inc. separately from all other money, and report their share of the total to the board every quarter. Doc 6 already forbids transfers as "capital".
3. Deliver programs directly. Grants to other bodies fall under the qualifying disbursement rules [L-44] and wait until counsel has advised.

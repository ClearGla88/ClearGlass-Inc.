# Program plan

> **Status notice.** ClearGlass Institute is a proposed organization. It is not yet incorporated and is not a registered charity. Files in this folder are working drafts and templates, not legal advice. Signed originals belong in the minute book, not in this repository.

Three programs. Nothing has been published or delivered. Figures on this page are targets.

## Program 1. Public AI and cyber literacy briefs

**What:** short explainers for people who are not specialists. First topics: recognizing phishing, what an AI model can and cannot be trusted with, how personal data gets exposed, and the ethics and limits of open-source research on people.

**Format:** 600 to 1,200 words. Plain language. Every factual claim cited to a source a reader can open. Dated. Author named. Free to read with no sign-up. Released under CC BY 4.0.

**Year-1 target:** 12 briefs.

**Template:** `BRIEF_TEMPLATE.md`.

## Program 2. Open defensive frameworks

**What:** one-page methodology cards that teach a way of thinking: how to reason about detection, what good logging looks like, a governance checklist for a small organization adopting AI tools.

**Boundary:** these are educational material. The Institute does not test anyone's systems, run assessments, or give advice on a specific organization's security. Requests for that work are declined, and the Institute does not pass them to ClearGlass Inc.

**Year-1 target:** 4 cards.

## Program 3. Student and community workshops

**What:** 60 to 90 minute sessions, in person or recorded, built on the briefs.

**Rule:** a library, college or community organization is named only after it has agreed in writing. The agreement goes in the minute book and the organization is added to `named_partners` in `data/status.json`. Until then it is not mentioned anywhere public, including in grant applications as a confirmed host.

**Year-1 target:** 4 workshops delivered, or 4 recorded modules.

## Publishing rules

1. **Sources.** Every factual claim has a source. Prefer primary sources: legislation, regulator guidance, standards bodies, peer-reviewed work, original incident reports. A vendor's marketing page is not a source.
2. **Second reader.** Someone other than the author opens every cited source and confirms it says what the brief says, before publication.
3. **Dates.** Each brief shows when it was published and when it was last reviewed. Review yearly, or sooner if the facts move.
4. **Corrections.** Errors are fixed in place with a dated note saying what changed.
5. **No product promotion.** No brief recommends a product or service of ClearGlass Inc., links to its sales pages, or collects contact details for it.
6. **No invented evidence.** No statistics, quotations, case studies or testimonials that cannot be traced to a source.
7. **Drafting help.** If software helped draft a brief, the named author still reads every sentence, checks every source, and is responsible for the result.
8. **Harm check.** Briefs explain how to recognize and defend against a threat. They do not give step-by-step instructions for carrying one out.

# ClearGlass Institute
## Ontario Not-for-Profit Corporation: Founding Kit

> **Status notice.** ClearGlass Institute is a proposed organization. It is not yet incorporated and is not a registered charity. Files in this folder are working drafts and templates, not legal advice. Signed originals belong in the minute book, not in this repository.

**Legal status of this document:** Draft operating charter and incorporation package. This is not a certificate of incorporation, a CRA registration, or proof of charitable status. Do not claim the Institute exists as a legal person until Ontario issues a certificate.

**Recommended legal name:** ClearGlass Institute (see the name risks in section 3 before paying for a search)
**Style name:** ClearGlass Institute (Public Interest)
**Jurisdiction:** Ontario, under the Not-for-Profit Corporations Act, 2010 (ONCA) [L-27]. Federal incorporation only if national reach makes it worth it later.
**Registered office (draft):** Burlington, Ontario, Canada
**Related for-profit (separate entity):** ClearGlass Inc. It must stay legally, financially and governance-separate. No shared bank accounts. Related-party transactions need board minutes and fair-market terms.

Rule references like [L-04] point to `LEGAL_VERIFICATION.md`, which lists each rule's sources and how far it was verified. Several rules are unverified. Read that file before relying on any of them.

## 1. Strategic decision

| Choice | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Entity type | Not-for-profit corporation under ONCA | Fastest Ontario path, legal personality, limited liability |
| Charity now? | No. Phase 2 | Incorporation is not charitable registration. Registration is a separate CRA application made online [L-21], and the review time is not guaranteed [L-22] |
| Tax receipts | None until the CRA registers the Institute as a charity | Only a registered charity or other qualified donee can issue official donation receipts [L-20] |
| Directors | Minimum 3 | ONCA minimum [L-01]. Every director must be an individual [L-02] |
| Public benefit corporation | Not a choice. Decided each year by where the money comes from | More than $10,000 a year in outside donations or government grants makes the Institute a public benefit corporation from the next financial year [L-04] |
| Surplus | Reinvested in the mission only | No dividends, no member profit |

**Open before filing: tax status in the unregistered period.** The purposes in section 2 are written as charitable purposes (advancement of education). The plan is to operate for a year or more without registering. The income tax exemption for non-profit organizations excludes an organization that is a charity in the CRA's opinion [L-23]. An unregistered organization with exclusively charitable purposes can fall between the two and be taxable. This rule is unverified here and needs a written opinion from a charity lawyer or CPA before the articles are filed. The answer may change the purposes clause, the timing of the charity application, or both.

**Charitable purposes later (CRA):** advancement of education, plus other purposes beneficial to the community (digital safety literacy). Do not mix commercial product sales into charitable objects.

## 2. Mission architecture

**Mission:** Advance public digital safety, AI literacy, and transparent systems knowledge so individuals, small organizations, and communities can understand and reduce digital risk.

**Vision:** A public that can evaluate AI, cybersecurity, and information systems without depending on vendor marketing.

**Public benefit, not member benefit:** Education, open frameworks, research summaries, and free public tools. Not a sales channel for ClearGlass Inc.

**Tagline:** Clarity in the public interest.

**Purposes clause (for the articles):**

1. To advance public education in cybersecurity, artificial intelligence literacy, digital investigations literacy, and information integrity.
2. To research, document, and publish non-commercial frameworks that help the public understand digital risk, system transparency, and defensive security practices.
3. To convene educational programs, workshops, and open resources for students, community organizations, and the general public in Canada.
4. To collaborate with educators, researchers, and civil-society groups on public-interest digital safety initiatives.
5. To do all things incidental and ancillary to the foregoing purposes, provided that the corporation shall not carry on any activity for the private profit of its members, directors, or related parties.

**Dissolution clause (needed for a future charity application):** Upon dissolution, remaining property after debts shall be distributed to one or more qualified donees under the Income Tax Act (Canada) with similar purposes, and not to members.

The dissolution clause is not settled. ONCA sets its own rules on who may receive the property, and they differ by the type of corporation [L-19]. Counsel must confirm wording that works under ONCA and for the CRA. See `articles/ARTICLES_DRAFT.md`.

## 3. What gets filed

**Filing:** Articles of Incorporation under ONCA (Form 5270), through the Ontario Business Registry or by mail [L-07].

**Government fee:** $155 per private guides. Confirm on the payment screen. Processing time is not settled; sources range from five business days to three weeks [L-07].

**Needed before filing:**

1. **Name search.** An Ontario-biased NUANS report, dated not more than 90 days before filing, from a private search house [L-08].
2. **Three directors** with legal names and addresses for service. Every director must be an individual, 18 or older, not bankrupt, and not found incapable. There is no residency requirement [L-01] [L-02]. Each signs a consent to act [L-03].
3. **Registered office address** in Ontario.
4. **Purposes** (section 2).
5. **Member classes:** one class of voting members. The first directors may be the first members.
6. **Special provisions:** no share capital, no gain for members, dissolution clause.

**Name risks. Settle these before buying the NUANS report.**

- **The word Institute.** Ontario restricts college, institute and university in a not-for-profit name where the word suggests a post-secondary institution, unless the Minister of Colleges and Universities consents in writing [L-09]. An educational body called an Institute is the case the rule is aimed at. The name may be refused or need that consent.
- **The word ClearGlass.** A name that can be confused with an existing corporation's can be refused, and the existing corporation's written consent may be needed [L-10]. If ClearGlass Inc. is on the register, expect to need its consent.
- **The first draft's fallback, ClearGlass Public Interest Institute, fixes neither problem.** It keeps both words. Fallbacks that work: drop Institute (for example ClearGlass Public Interest Centre) if that word is the obstacle; take a number name and operate under a registered style name if the search fails [L-11].

**Within 60 days after the certificate:**

- File the initial return [L-12].
- Pass the Institute's own by-laws at the organizational meeting. If none are passed in 60 days, the Ministry's standard by-laws apply by default [L-18]. By-laws passed by directors last only until the next members' meeting unless members confirm them [L-17].

**Also after the certificate:**

- Organizational meeting minutes (appoint officers, adopt by-laws, approve the banking resolution)
- Minute book: articles, by-laws, directors register, members register, consents to act
- CRA business number. Check whether one came with the incorporation; if not, register for one [L-26]
- Bank account in the Institute's legal name only
- Domain and code host separate from ClearGlass Inc.

**Do not:** claim to be a registered charity, issue tax receipts, mix ClearGlass Inc. funds with the Institute's, or list partners or customers that have not agreed in writing.

## 4. Governance

**Board (minimum 3):**

| Seat | Role | Independence rule |
|---|---|---|
| Chair | Independent if possible | Not a controlling shareholder of ClearGlass Inc., for grant and charity credibility |
| Treasurer | Financial control | Two signatures on payments above a set threshold |
| Secretary | Records and registry filings | Owns the filing calendar |

**Officers:** Executive Director (unpaid at first), Treasurer, Secretary. The founder may serve as Executive Director or Chair, not both once grants or a charity application are in play.

If the Institute becomes a public benefit corporation, the number of directors who are employees of the Institute or its affiliates is capped [L-05]. Check the cap before putting any director on payroll.

**Conflict policy (mandatory because of ClearGlass Inc.):**

- Any contract with ClearGlass Inc. needs the conflicted directors to step out and a written fair-market rationale.
- Institute material for public education is released under CC BY or a similar licence. Commercial product IP stays in the Inc.
- No exclusive licence that takes public-benefit use away from the Institute.

Full text: `governance/CONFLICT_AND_RELATED_PARTY_POLICY.md`.

**Meetings:** Board at least quarterly. First annual members' meeting within 18 months of incorporation, then at least every 15 months [L-15]. Plan each annual meeting within six months after the year end [L-16].

**Year-1 controls:**

- Two people control the bank account
- Initial return within 60 days [L-12]; annual return within six months after each year end [L-13]; notice of change within 15 days of any change in directors, officers or address [L-14]
- T2 return each year even with no tax owing [L-25]; T1044 if its tests are met [L-24]
- Simple cash accounting until revenue needs an accountant
- Audit and review engagement: members decide each year, within the limits for the Institute's revenue and status [L-06]

## 7. Money

**Early revenue open to a non-profit:** membership dues if any, program fees at cost, sponsorships with disclosure, grants that do not require a qualified donee.

**Not allowed:** distributing profit; treating Inc. invoices as Institute expenses without board approval.

**Watch the $10,000 line.** Outside donations or government grants above $10,000 in a financial year make the Institute a public benefit corporation from the following year [L-04]. That changes the audit rules [L-06], the employee-director cap [L-05], and where property can go on dissolution [L-19].

**Charity trigger:** the Institute needs official donation receipts, or a funder requires qualified donee status. Then: lawyer review of the purposes, online application to the CRA [L-21], and a wait that no source could put a firm number on [L-22].

**Separation from ClearGlass Inc.:** the Institute educates; the Inc. builds products. Shared branding is a risk. Use the word Institute in every public sentence about it.

2. Three directors' consents on file
3. By-laws adopted in minutes
4. CRA business number issued
